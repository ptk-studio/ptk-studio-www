Printable Graph Paper Actual Size, Without Shrinking

November 16, 2026 · 4 min read

Tags: printables, how-to

Graph paper is only useful if a square measures what it claims to. Printers and PDF viewers often scale a page down without telling you, so 5 mm squares quietly become 4.7 mm. This post shows how to print 5 mm or 1/4 inch graph paper at actual size, and how to prove it with a ruler.

Why graph paper shrinks when you print

Shrinking is usually caused by a setting, not by a faulty printer. Three things are the most common culprits:

  • Fit to page or Shrink to fit. Many print dialogs and PDF viewers choose this by default. It scales the whole page so everything lands inside the printable area, and the grid scales with it.
  • Paper size mismatch. US Letter is 8.5 x 11 inches (215.9 x 279.4 mm). A4 is 210 x 297 mm. If a file made for one is sent to the other, the software may rescale it to fit.
  • Unprintable edges. Many printers cannot print right to the edge of the sheet. A grid that runs to the edge may be clipped or, again, scaled down to make room.

Step 1: Match the grid to your paper

Decide which unit you work in. Metric users usually want 5 mm squares on A4. US users usually want 1/4 inch squares on Letter. Then make sure the file you print is built for the paper that is actually in the tray. A grid made for A4 and printed on Letter is the classic way to end up with a scaled page.

Step 2: Print at 100%

The exact wording differs between programs, but the steps are the same:

  1. Open the file and choose Print.
  2. Find the scale setting. Pick Actual size or 100%.
  3. Turn off anything called Fit, Shrink oversized pages, or Fit to printable area.
  4. Check that the paper size in the dialog matches the paper in the printer.
  5. If your printer driver has its own scaling option, set that to 100% too. Some drivers apply a second scale on top of the program's setting.
  6. Print one page, not a stack.

Step 3: Check it with a ruler

Do not trust the look of the page. Measure it. Lay a ruler along a row of squares and measure across ten of them. Ten squares should span 50 mm for a 5 mm grid, or 2.5 inches for a 1/4 inch grid. Measuring ten squares rather than one makes a small error much easier to see.

If the result is short, divide what you measured by what you expected. Measuring 48 mm where you expected 50 mm means the page printed at about 96%. That points to a scaling setting you missed. Go back to Step 2 and look for a second one in the printer driver.

A way that needs no special tool

You can build a grid in a word processor. Insert a table with as many columns and rows as fit on the page. Select the whole table and set the row height to an exact value, 5 mm or 0.25 inch, and the column width to the same value. Use the option that says the height is exactly that value, not at least. Set thin borders, then print at 100% and measure as above. It takes a few minutes and works with software you already have, though you will have to repeat it for each paper size.

Quick checklist

  • Grid size chosen: 5 mm or 1/4 inch.
  • File paper size matches the paper in the tray (Letter or A4).
  • Scale set to Actual size or 100%.
  • Fit to page and Shrink to fit turned off, in the program and in the printer driver.
  • One test page printed.
  • Ten squares measured: 50 mm or 2.5 inches.
  • If short, divide measured by expected to find the scale, then look for the setting causing it.
