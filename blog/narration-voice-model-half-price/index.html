Can you hear the difference? A blind test of two AI narration models

November 18, 2026 · 2 min read

Tags: AI models, blind tests, AI voice, YouTube, cost

Every scene in our HoneyStorm History videos is narrated by the same AI voice. ElevenLabs offers a cheaper, faster model that can use the same voice, so we asked: can the person who runs the channel tell them apart without knowing which is which? This is a small test (one clip, one judge).

How we tested

  • Same text, same voice, different models. Both models read exactly the same text with the same voice and settings. No re-rolls, no cherry-picking: the first clip each model returned is the one shown.
  • Blind. The two clips were labelled only A and B, in a random order. The judge, the person who runs the channels, picked a favourite before the models were revealed.
  • Real costs. Prices are what we were actually billed per clip that day. They're not list prices, and they will change.

The test

The job: the same 17-second opening of the Dyatlov script, read by the same voice, with two ElevenLabs models.

The judge: "very close." When the judge can't tell, the cheaper one wins, so our narration moves to Flash v2.5, at half the cost with the same voice.

What we changed

History narration moves to ElevenLabs Flash v2.5 with the same voice: half the cost. A 20-scene video's narration drops from about $0.70 to about $0.35.

What we learned

  1. When it's "very close", take the cheaper one. Viewers won't notice a difference the channel's own maker can barely hear.
  2. Keep the voice, change the engine. Using the same voice meant the channel's narrator didn't change.
  3. Quality has to show up in the numbers. So we're running a follow-up: about 75 % of our next history videos are made with the cheaper models and 25 % with the original ones, picked at random. After enough are published, we compare watch time, retention and click-through by group. If the more expensive models don't earn its cost in viewer behaviour, it goes. We'll report the result here.
