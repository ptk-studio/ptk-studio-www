For realistic people, our blind test kept the pricier image model

November 18, 2026 · 3 min read

Tags: ai-models, blind-tests, ai-images

Every song on our music channels, Our Stories in Melody and Lantern Light, gets a photorealistic cover image. An hour earlier, the cheapest model had won our painted-scene test, so we asked whether it also handles realistic people. This is a small test (two covers, one judge), so treat it as a practical read, not a benchmark.

How we tested

  • Same prompt, different models. Every model got exactly the same prompt, aspect ratio and settings. No re-rolls, no cherry-picking: the first image each model returned is the one shown.
  • Blind. The images were placed side by side in a random order, labelled only X, Y and Z. The judge, the person who runs the channels, picked a favourite before the models were revealed.
  • Real costs. Prices are what we were actually billed per image that day. They're not list prices, and they will change.

The test

The job: photorealistic album covers. One shows a young woman writing in a café notebook. The other shows a couple on a city rooftop at blue hour, sharing one pair of earphones. Hands, faces and earphone wires are where image models usually slip.

The judge: "Y is the best, then X, then Z."

What we noticed: all three handled faces, hands and the earphone wires well, a big step up from what we saw on our older covers. The judge preferred the Nano Banana pair for realistic people. Seedream, the winner for painted scenes, came last here.

What we changed

Nothing. Covers stay on Nano Banana 2, and when Google retires it, they move to Nano Banana 2.1, the judge's second choice. A cover is one image per song, so the better model costs about 5 cents more per song, which is worth it on the image people click.

What we learned

  1. "Cheaper" isn't one answer. The model that won on painted scenes came last on realistic faces.
  2. Hands and wires are fine now. All three handled the faces, hands and earphone wires that older models used to get wrong.
