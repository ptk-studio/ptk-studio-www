Mochi QuestPop

Privacy Policy

Last updated 11 September 2026

The short version. You can play without registering — the app makes you a guest account by itself and never asks for your email. Quests you publish are stored on our server so the people you shared them with can play them. Puzzle answers and gift-card codes are kept in a place no app can read, including yours. The app measures which kinds of puzzle get played and whether quests get finished; it never sends anything you typed, any answer, any share code, or your location.

Who we are

Mochi QuestPop is made by PTK Studio. This policy covers the Mochi QuestPop iOS app and this page. If anything here is unclear, write to hello@ptk-studio.com and we’ll answer plainly.

Your account

The first time you open the app it signs you in as an anonymous guest. That account is a random identifier and nothing else — no email, no name, no phone number — and it exists so the quest you are halfway through is still there tomorrow. You are never asked to register in order to play.

Making a quest is different, because a quest is something other people will open, so it needs an account that survives losing the phone. That is Sign in with Google, and it is the only sign-in we offer. When you use it, your guest account is upgraded in place so the progress you already made carries over. We receive your Google account identifier and the display name on it. We store the display name, because it appears on quests you publish. We do not store your email address — it stays with the sign-in provider and is never written into our database, attached to analytics, or used to contact you.

What is stored on our server

QuestPop is a thing you share with other people, so most of it has to live somewhere both of you can reach:

  • Quests you make — the title, description, cover image, stages, story text, clues, puzzles, and how the ending works.
  • Images and audio you upload for a cover, a stage, a clue or an ending, held in Cloud Storage under a random file name.
  • Your progress in each quest — which puzzles you have solved, how many attempts each took, which hints you opened, when you started and finished, and your score.
  • Who is in a private quest, so the right people can open it.
  • Your profile — display name, notification preferences, and the creators you have blocked.

Your progress is readable by you and by nobody else. The person who made the quest cannot see how many attempts you took, and neither can anyone else playing it.

The two things kept where no app can read them

Some data is deliberately put out of reach of every copy of the app, including yours:

  • Puzzle answers. When a quest is published its answers are moved into a separate store that no app is permitted to read. Submitting an answer sends what you entered to our server, which compares it and replies only with right or wrong. This is why you cannot get ahead by inspecting the app, and it is also why we are able to say that an answer is never sent anywhere else.
  • Gift-card codes. If a quest ends in a gift card, the code is encrypted with a managed key before it is stored and is never written into the quest itself. It is decrypted only for the one person it was addressed to, only after they have finished the quest, and only once the creator has released it. It is never put into a link, a notification, an analytics event or a crash report, and the creator cannot read it back afterwards — the app shows them only the last four characters.

QuestPop does not sell, issue or buy gift cards. A creator can only attach a code they already own, and only to a private quest with a named recipient.

What the app reports

QuestPop uses Firebase Analytics to count how the app is used, so we can tell which parts are worth improving. Every event is one of a fixed list, and every value is a fixed option, a count, or an identifier that means nothing outside our database — for example that a combination lock was attempted for the third time, that a quest with seven stages was started, or that a wide-screen layout was used. Durations are reported as a band rather than a number, so a play session cannot be used to pick one person out.

The app also sends crash reports through Firebase Crashlytics: a stack trace, the iOS version and the device model, plus a note of which operation was in flight — “submitting an answer”, not what the answer was.

Firebase App Check uses Apple’s App Attest to confirm that requests are coming from a real copy of our app rather than a script. It tells us nothing about you.

You can turn usage reporting off in Settings → Privacy → Share anonymous usage data.

What is never reported

  • Anything you typed. Quest titles, stories, clues, riddles, messages you wrote for someone, names you gave things. The app is built so that a measurement has nowhere to put free text even by mistake.
  • Puzzle answers, yours or the creator’s.
  • Join codes. We record that a code was used, never which one.
  • Anything about a gift card — the code, the PIN, the barcode, the brand, the value, or who it was for.
  • Your location, precise or otherwise. The app does not use location services at all. A quest can tell you to walk somewhere; the app does not watch you do it.
  • Your email address, phone number or contacts.
  • Your photo library. Choosing a cover image uses the system picker, which hands the app the one picture you chose and nothing else — which is why the app never asks for photo access.
  • Your camera, calendar, or health data.
  • Your advertising identifier. There are no ads and no advertising network, you will never see an App Tracking Transparency prompt, and nothing about you is combined with data from other companies.

Permissions, and when they are asked for

QuestPop asks for one thing, and only when it becomes relevant:

  • Notifications — requested the first time you start a quest that releases a stage on a schedule, never at first launch. If you decline, that quest still works; you just have to remember to come back. You can change it in Settings → Notifications.

Notifications are scheduled on your phone rather than sent from our server, and they are deliberately vague — “Your next QuestPop stage is ready” — because a lock screen is visible to whoever is standing next to it and a countdown quest is usually meant to be a surprise.

Quests other people made

Anyone can publish a quest, which means you may open text and pictures we did not write. A basic word check runs before a quest is published, and we are clear in the app that this is a first pass rather than moderation. Every quest can be reported from its own page, and you can block a creator, which hides everything of theirs from you.

A report records which quest, the reason you picked, anything you added, and who sent it. The creator cannot read reports and cannot find out who reported them.

Who else touches it

Google, as the provider of Firebase, stores the data described above on our behalf and processes the anonymous usage counts and crash reports. Google also provides Sign in with Google. There is no advertising network, no analytics broker, and no other server of ours. No data of yours is sold.

Children

Mochi QuestPop is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. There is no chat and no user-to-user messaging; the only text another person can put in front of you is the quest they wrote, and any quest can be reported or its creator blocked.

Your choices and rights

  • Play without an account. Public quests never ask you to register.
  • Turn usage reporting off in Settings.
  • Take a quest down that you published, at any time.
  • Delete your account in Settings → Delete account. This removes your profile, your progress everywhere, your membership of other people’s quests, and every quest you made — including for the people who were playing them. Any report you sent stays, with your identity stripped from it, so that moderation still works. It cannot be undone.
  • Ask us anything at hello@ptk-studio.com. Depending on where you live you may have rights to access, correct, or delete your personal information, and we honour those requests regardless of where you live.

Changes to this policy

If we change what the app collects, we’ll update this page and change the date at the top before the change ships. Material changes will also be noted in the release notes.
