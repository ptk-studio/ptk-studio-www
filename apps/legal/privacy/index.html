Mochi Legal

Privacy Policy

Last updated 26 August 2026

The short version. Mochi Legal asks you nothing and has no account. The only thing it keeps is the list of pages you bookmarked, and that list never leaves your device. Two things are sent, both switchable in Settings: a crash report if the app crashes, and — only if you turn it on — a count of which content page was opened. Nothing you read, saved, or typed is ever transmitted, because there is nowhere in the app to type anything.

Who we are

Mochi Legal is made by PTK Studio. This policy covers the Mochi Legal iOS app and this page. If anything here is unclear, write to hello@ptk-studio.com and we will answer plainly.

What stays on your device

Your bookmarks, and your settings. That is the complete list. A bookmark is stored as an identifier for a page that ships inside the app — a short code like divorce or ev-deadlines — in the app's own storage. We have no copy of it and cannot read it. Deleting the app deletes it.

There is no free text anywhere in the app: no notes, no documents, no case numbers, no party names, no dates, and no search box. There is no field in which anyone could enter a name, an address or an account number, because no such field exists.

What is sent, and only what is sent

Two third-party SDKs are linked, each confined to a single file, and neither is given anything that identifies you.

  • Crash reports — on by default, and you can turn them off. If the app crashes, Firebase Crashlytics sends the stack trace and the state of the app at the moment it failed, along with your device model and iOS version so the failure can be reproduced. It does not include what you were reading. Turn it off under Settings → Send crash reports.
  • Page counts — off until you turn them on. If you switch on Help us pick what to write next, Statsig receives the identifier of a content page when it is opened — the same short code that a bookmark uses, from a list that ships inside the app. It is how we decide what to write next. It is not linked to any identity: the app constructs its analytics user with no user id and no custom ids, and an automated test fails the build if anyone ever adds one.

Neither is used for advertising, neither is combined with data from other companies' apps or websites, and there is no advertising identifier in the build — which is why you will never see an App Tracking Transparency prompt.

What we never collect

  • Your name, email address, phone number, or any account — there is no sign-up.
  • Your contacts, photos, calendar, microphone, or camera.
  • Your location, precise or otherwise. The app never asks where you live, which is also why it has no jurisdiction picker.
  • Anything you searched for. There is no search.
  • Which state, situation, or topic a particular person is interested in. The page counts described above are totals with nobody attached to them.

Opening official pages

The app links to courts, statutes and government agencies throughout. Those open in an in-app Safari view, which runs in its own process: it shows you the real web address, and the app has no access to that browsing, its history, its cookies or its autofill data. Where you tap a telephone number — a domestic violence hotline, for instance — it goes to your dialler rather than to a web page.

Those sites are not ours and have their own privacy policies. We are not told that you visited one.

Reading this on a shared device

A share of the people reading about eviction or domestic violence are doing it on a phone someone else can pick up, so three things are built for that. App Lock asks for Face ID or Touch ID when the app reopens. Hide in app switcher, on by default, covers the screen so what you were reading is not visible in multitasking. The Leave button closes everything open in the app at once.

None of that reaches outside the app: pages you opened in the in-app browser are not part of it, and clearing your browser history is a separate step.

Children

The app is not directed at children.

Your rights

Laws such as the GDPR and the CCPA give you rights to access, correct, export and delete personal data a company holds about you. We hold no personal data about you — the crash reports and page counts described above are not tied to a person and cannot be traced back to one — so there is nothing for us to produce or erase. Everything the app stores is on your own device and under your control.

Changes

If what the app collects ever changes, this page, the wording inside the app, and its App Store privacy disclosure will all change before that version ships — not after.

Not legal advice

Mochi Legal provides general legal information. It is not legal advice, it does not create an attorney-client relationship, and it is not a substitute for a licensed attorney or a court self-help center.
