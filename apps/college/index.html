Mochi College

Every US college, in your pocket.

Costs, admission rates, majors, class sizes and what graduates go on to earn — drawn from the federal College Scorecard. The whole dataset ships inside the app, so searching it needs no signal and tells no one what you looked at.

What it is

  • The federal dataset, made browsable

    6,429 institutions, every one that reports to the U.S. Department of Education — research universities, liberal arts colleges, community colleges and trade schools alike. Not a curated shortlist of the famous ones.

  • Nothing is fetched while you browse

    Every figure is stored on the device. The app is as useful on a plane as it is at a kitchen table, and a search is never a request to a server — nobody, ourselves included, can see which schools you opened.

  • Filter by what actually decides it

    Narrow by state, tuition, acceptance rate, enrolment, SAT range, campus setting and whether a school is public, private or for-profit — or show only test-optional schools, HBCUs, or women's colleges. Then sort what's left by cost, selectivity, graduation rate or what graduates earn.

  • Three ways in when you have no name to type

    Browse by state, browse by major — grouped into fields, so you can start from what you want to study — or open a curated list: best value public schools, hidden gems, test-optional schools, top HBCUs, highest earning graduates.

  • Build a list you can annotate

    Save a school and it lands in My List, where each one takes a note — a deadline, a campus visit, why it caught your eye. Your list stays on your device.

  • What isn't here

    No ads. No account, no sign-up, no password. No paywall in front of the data, and nothing nagging you to come back.

About the data

Figures come from the U.S. Department of Education's College Scorecard, the public dataset colleges are required to report into, as published in its April 2025 release — 6,429 institutions and 219,079 program records. Because the reporting cycle runs behind the academic year, treat the numbers as the shape of a school rather than a quote: costs in particular are what was last reported, not what you will be billed.
