Mochi NetProbe

Privacy Policy

Last updated 14 November 2026

The short version. NetProbe looks at the network you are already joined to. Everything it finds — the devices, the names you give them, your notes and your scan history — stays on your phone. There is no account and no server of ours. We do not know what is on your network, and we have no way to find out. Since version 1.1 the app sends anonymous usage counts and crash reports through Google's Firebase, so we can see which tools get used and fix what breaks; this page says exactly what those are, and none of it is about your network.

Who we are

Mochi NetProbe is made by Mochi Factory, run by PTK Studio. This policy covers the Mochi NetProbe iOS app and this page. If anything here is unclear, write to hello@mochi-factory.com and we'll answer plainly.

What stays on your device

All of it. Everything the app produces lives in its own storage on your phone:

  • The devices each scan found — their addresses, hardware addresses, names, open ports and everything identified about them.
  • Your scan history per network, which is how the app can tell you a device is new.
  • Any name or note you attach to a device.
  • Your settings.

We have no copy of any of it and we cannot read it. Deleting the app deletes it.

What we collect

Anonymous usage counts and crash reports, through two Google services, and nothing that identifies you or describes your network:

  • Firebase Analytics — that a scan started, finished or was cancelled, with the number of devices found rounded into a range (none, 1, up to 5, up to 10, up to 25, up to 50, more); that a tool was opened (which one: ping, traceroute, port scan, DNS, WHOIS, Wake-on-LAN, Bonjour, interfaces, known networks); that a device's detail screen was opened or a scan exported — never which device. Along with these, Firebase records the device model, iOS version, app version and the country your connection appears to come from, and assigns a random identifier to the installation, not to you.
  • Firebase Crashlytics — if the app crashes, a report of where in the code it failed, with the device model, iOS version and app version, so we can fix it.

This is worth stating plainly rather than burying, because a network scanner is exactly the kind of app where it would matter: none of it contains an address, a hardware address, a device name, a network name, a port, a banner, a note, or anything you type. We still do not know what is on your network or what you named anything.

What we never collect

  • Your name, email address, phone number, or any account — there is no sign-up.
  • Your contacts, photos, calendar, microphone, or camera.
  • Your precise location. The app never asks for it. (Firebase Analytics works out the country from your connection's address, as noted above.)
  • Your advertising identifier. The app does not ask to track you across other companies' apps and websites, so you will never see an App Tracking Transparency prompt.

Permissions, and when they are asked for

NetProbe asks for one thing:

  • Local network access — requested the first time you scan, because finding the devices on your network is the whole app. iOS shows this prompt itself. The permission lets the app talk to other devices on the network you are joined to; it does not grant access to your files, your accounts, or anything on the internet.

That is the complete list. The app does not ask for location, camera, photos, contacts, microphone, or notifications.

What the app sends over your network

During a scan, NetProbe sends ordinary network requests to the devices on your own network and reads what they send back — the same kinds of request a browser, a printer driver or a music app already makes. It asks each device what it will freely say about itself. It does not attempt to log in, does not send passwords, and does not change any setting on any device.

Nothing about a scan leaves your network. Scan results are never uploaded, because there is nowhere for them to go.

What reaches the internet

The anonymous usage counts and crash reports described above go to Google's Firebase. Beyond that, two features in the Tools tab deliberately go beyond your own network. Neither runs during a scan, and neither runs unless you tap it:

  • WHOIS lookup. The domain or address you type is sent to the public WHOIS system — first to IANA, then to whichever registry IANA points at — which is how a WHOIS lookup works anywhere. Those operators see the query and your IP address. Only what you type is sent.
  • Public IP address. If you tap "Look up" on the network card, the app makes one request to ipify.org, a free service whose only job is to reply with the address your request came from. It sends nothing about you or your devices; the address is simply what any web server would already see.

Neither result is stored anywhere but on your phone, and neither is sent to us.

Who else touches it

Google (Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics) receives the usage counts and crash reports described above, as our service provider, under its own terms. No data of yours is sold or shared with anyone else. The two lookups above talk to public internet infrastructure at your request. Apple knows you downloaded the app, as it does for every app, and passes us download counts and, from people who chose to share them, crash reports, none of which name anybody.

Children

Mochi NetProbe is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them — we do not collect personal information from anyone. There is no chat, no user-to-user messaging, and no public content in the app.

Your choices and rights

  • Clear your history in Tools → Known networks, which forgets every remembered network, the names you gave devices, and your notes.
  • Delete everything by deleting the app.
  • Ask us anything at hello@mochi-factory.com. Depending on where you live you may have rights to access, correct, or delete your personal information. We honour those requests regardless of where you live — though in NetProbe's case the honest answer is that we hold nothing that identifies you: your scan results never leave your phone, and the usage counts and crash reports aren't linked to you, so we can't look one person's up.

Feedback

If you send us feedback in Mochi NetProbe (through the feedback button, form or app screen), we receive your message, the kind of feedback you chose, an optional star rating, the page or app version you were using, and your language setting. We ask for your email only if you want a reply, and we keep it only if you tick the box allowing us to contact you; we delete it after at most 90 days. We don't use cookies or tracking for feedback, and we don't collect device identifiers. We also store the IP address your feedback was sent from, to prevent abuse; it is deleted with your feedback after one year. Feedback is stored by PTK Studio's 88eggs service (hosted in the United States), used only to fix problems and improve Mochi NetProbe, never sold or shared, and deleted after one year. To have your feedback deleted sooner, contact us at hello@mochi-factory.com.

Changes to this policy

If we change what the app collects, we'll update this page and change the date at the top before the change ships. Material changes will also be noted in the release notes.
