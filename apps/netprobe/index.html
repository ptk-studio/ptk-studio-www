Mochi NetProbe

See everything on your network.

Tap scan and NetProbe finds every device sharing your Wi-Fi — not as a list of numbers, but by name: the printer, the thermostat, the TV, the one you don't recognise. For anything with a web interface, it will also tell you what software is running behind it.

What it is

  • An inventory that reads like one

    Not 192.168.0.94 but Brother MFC-7860DW — with its manufacturer, model, and the fact that it accepts print jobs three different ways. NetProbe asks the network five different ways at once, because no single method finds everything.

  • It tells you what changed

    Anything that wasn't there last time you scanned is marked new. Name a device once — "Rachel's iPad", "the garage camera" — and the name sticks, so next time you're only looking at what you haven't already accounted for.

  • What that box is running

    For devices with a web interface, NetProbe reads the response the way a developer would: the web server and its version, the language and framework behind it, the TLS certificate and when it expires. Routers, NAS boxes and cameras all give this up readily.

  • It shows its working

    Every conclusion carries the evidence it came from and how strongly it's held — declared when the server named itself, likely when it's inferred from a cookie or a header, possible when it's a hint in the page. A guess is never dressed up as a fact.

  • The usual tools, properly done

    Ping, traceroute, port scan, DNS lookup, WHOIS, Wake-on-LAN, a Bonjour browser, and a plain view of your interfaces and routes. Custom address ranges when you need to look somewhere other than your own subnet.

  • It stays on your phone

    The scan runs on the device and the results stay there. No account, no sign-up, no analytics — we don't know how many devices you have or that you opened the app. Two features reach the internet, WHOIS and the public-IP check, and both only when you tap them.

What it isn't

NetProbe reads; it doesn't reach in. It asks each device what it will freely say about itself and reports the answer. It doesn't try passwords, doesn't exploit anything, and can't block a device from the network — no iOS app can, whatever the App Store description says. It's a tool for understanding a network you're responsible for.
